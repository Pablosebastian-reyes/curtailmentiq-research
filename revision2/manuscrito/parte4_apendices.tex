\section{Inversion of the PIT limit}
\label{app:inversion}

Fix $x$ and write $p=p(x)$, $\mu=\mu(x)$. The mixture CDF is $F_x(y)=0$ for $y<0$, $F_x(0)=1-p$, and $F_x(y)=(1-p)+p\,\Phi\big((\log y-\mu)/\sigma\big)$ for $y>0$, which is continuous and strictly increasing on $(0,\infty)$ from $1-p$ to $1$. Given a level $\hat q\in[0,1]$ we seek the smallest $u\ge 0$ with $F_x(u)\ge \hat q$. If $\hat q\le 1-p$ the atom already reaches the level, so $u=0$. If $\hat q\ge 1$ no finite $u$ attains it and $u=+\infty$. For $1-p<\hat q<1$, strict monotonicity gives a unique solution of $(1-p)+p\,\Phi\big((\log u-\mu)/\sigma\big)=\hat q$, hence $\log u=\mu+\sigma\,\Phi^{-1}\big((\hat q-(1-p))/p\big)$, which is~\eqref{eq:invert}. The boundary cases are the limits of the clipped argument, since $\Phi^{-1}(0^+)=-\infty$ gives $U=0$ and $\Phi^{-1}(1^-)=+\infty$ gives $U=+\infty$. \qed

For the quantile model the same object is obtained without a closed form. Let $\tau_1<\dots<\tau_K$ be the estimated levels and $\hat q_{\tau_1}(x)\le\dots\le\hat q_{\tau_K}(x)$ the rearranged quantiles, and augment the grid with the node $(0,0)$. Then $F_x$ is the piecewise-linear interpolant of the augmented pairs, its atom is $F_x(0)=\max\{\tau_k:\hat q_{\tau_k}(x)=0\}$, and $F_x^{-1}(\hat q)$ is the piecewise-linear interpolant in the other direction, equal to $0$ for $\hat q\le F_x(0)$ and to $+\infty$ for $\hat q>\tau_K$.

For both families, and for $y>0$, $y\le F_x^{-1}(q)$ if and only if $F_x(y)\le q$. The coverage of a constant threshold $q$ over a set of plant-days is therefore the fraction of zero outcomes plus the fraction of positive outcomes with $F_x(y)\le q$, which is how the oracle of Section~\ref{sec:decomp} is computed exactly.

\section{Coverage under score transport}
\label{app:coverage}

\emph{Static split.} If the calibration scores $s_1,\dots,s_n$ and the test score $s_{n+1}$ are exchangeable, then with $\hat q=s_{(k)}$, $k=\lceil(n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil$, one has $\mathbb{P}(s_{n+1}\le\hat q)\ge k/(n+1)\ge 1-\alpha$, and since $F_x$ is monotone, $s_{n+1}\le\hat q$ if and only if $Y\le U(x)$; hence $\mathbb{P}(Y\le U(x))\ge 1-\alpha$. The statement is marginal and exact only under exchangeability; with an intraclass correlation by date of 0.238 and 108.8 plants per day, the effective calibration sample is about 1{,}000 rather than 26{,}552, and the guarantee should be read at that size.

\emph{Transported scores.} Let $P_{\mathrm{old}}$ and $P_{\mathrm{new}}$ be the laws of the score in the old and new regimes and $T=F_{\mathrm{new}}^{-1}\circ F_{\mathrm{old}}$ the monotone transport map. Under an oracle map, $T(s)\sim P_{\mathrm{new}}$ for $s\sim P_{\mathrm{old}}$, so the transported calibration scores are exchangeable with the test score and the static guarantee transfers. With an estimated map $\hat T$ the coverage gap is bounded by the total-variation distance $d_{\mathrm{TV}}\big(\hat T_\# P_{\mathrm{old}},P_{\mathrm{new}}\big)$ through the framework of Barber et al.~\cite{barber2023}. The bound cannot be computed from data, so validity is obtained by feeding the transported scores into the online update of Gibbs and Cand\`es~\cite{gibbs2021}, whose long-run coverage holds for any sequence; the transport contributes sharpness and the update contributes coverage. Section~\ref{sec:ablaciones} shows that on these data the sharpness contribution is negative once the update is present.

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

Every table, figure and number in the text is regenerated by versioned scripts from the frozen dataset; the numbers quoted in the text are read from the result files by a script rather than transcribed. The environment is Python 3.12.3 with pandas 3.0.3, numpy 2.4.6, scipy 1.17.1, scikit-learn 1.9.0, xgboost 3.2.0, ruptures 1.1.10, statsmodels 0.15.0 and matplotlib 3.11.0. The seeds are 42 for base-model training, 20260720 for the randomisation of the PIT atom and the bootstrap of the transport map, 11 for the CRPS sampling, 20260901 for the day-block bootstraps, the permutation nulls and the wild cluster bootstrap, and 20261103 for the simulation. The order in which the conformal generator is consumed is fixed and documented, so that the reference hurdle reproduces its published results bit for bit. All nine base models were refitted from the training data with the published code: the reference models reproduce the stored predictions to a relative difference below $10^{-5}$, which is the rounding of the stored files, and the early-stopped hurdle reproduces its tree counts and dispersion exactly. The command sequence and a log of every run are in the companion repository.

\section{Specification of the controlled simulation}
\label{app:sim}

\emph{Panel.} $N=100$ plants observed for 240 calibration days followed by 638 test days, split into windows of 30, 92, 181, 184 and 151 days, the lengths of the real windows; the second window plays the role of the transition window.

\emph{Data-generating process.} For plant $i$ and day $t$, occurrence is $Z_{it}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}\big(\mathrm{logit}^{-1}(a_i+\eta_t)\big)$ and, when $Z_{it}=1$, $\log Y_{it}=m_i+\ell_t+\xi_t+s_t\,\varepsilon_{it}$ with $\varepsilon_{it}\sim N(0,1)$. Plant effects are $a_i\sim N(1,0.8^2)$ and $m_i\sim N(4,0.8^2)$. The common shocks $\eta_t$ and $\xi_t$ are stationary first-order autoregressive processes with coefficient 0.7 and marginal standard deviations 0.8 and 0.5. They are the source of dependence across plants.

\emph{Scenarios.} The level path $\ell_t$ and the idiosyncratic dispersion $s_t$ are constant at $0$ and $1$ before the transition window, change linearly over it and stay constant afterwards. S0: no change. S1: $\ell$ rises by $\log 3$, a tripling of magnitudes. S2: as S1, and $s$ falls from 1.0 to 0.6. S3: $s$ falls from 1.0 to 0.6 with no change in level.

\emph{Base predictives.} All are lognormal hurdles with occurrence probability equal to the true probability marginalised over $\eta_t$, which a forecaster seven days ahead cannot predict. They differ in location and dispersion. Writing $\sigma_t=(s_t^2+0.5^2)^{1/2}$ for the true marginal dispersion of $\log Y$ given occurrence and $\sigma_{\mathrm{cal}}$ for its calibration value, the predictive dispersion is $\kappa\,\sigma_{\mathrm{cal}}^{1-\phi}\sigma_t^{\phi}$ and the location is $m_i+\ell_{t-L}$. M1, the oracle: $\phi=1$, $\kappa=1$, $L=0$. M2: $\phi=1$, $\kappa=1.3$. M3: $\phi=0.5$. M4, constant dispersion: $\phi=0$. M5: $\phi=0$, $\kappa=1.3$. M6: $\phi=0$, $\kappa=0.8$. M7: $\phi=1$, $L=30$ days. M8: $\phi=0$, $L=30$ days.

\emph{Calibration and evaluation.} The same code as for the real data: static PIT split on the calibration days; ACI and Transport+ACI with $\gamma=0.05$, $\alpha_{\min}=0.005$, a 60-day window, weekly refresh and the 7-day embargo; nominal level 0.90. For each model-window cell we compute the static over-coverage, the width change of each adaptive layer and the width change of the constant-threshold oracle at the adaptive layer's coverage. Fifty independent replicates per scenario. The seeds of the data, of the randomisation of the PIT atom and of the map bootstrap are fixed functions of the scenario, replicate and model indices, so every replicate can be regenerated on its own.
